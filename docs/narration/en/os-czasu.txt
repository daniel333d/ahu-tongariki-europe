Timeline.

About a thousand years ago. Settlers from eastern Polynesia reach Rapa Nui.

11th-17th century. Development of the great ahu complexes and the moai-carving tradition.

Later pre-colonial period. Social and religious change; Orongo and the Tangata Manu ceremony grow in importance.

1722. First well-documented encounter between the islanders and a European expedition.

1862-1863. Slave raids and abductions; followed by epidemics and a dramatic fall in population.

1888. Signing of documents between representatives of Chile and the leaders of Rapa Nui.

From 1895. Nearly six decades of sheep-farming dominance and confinement of islanders on their own land.

1995. Rapa Nui National Park is inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

Today. Language, craft, music, dance, Kai-kai and genealogical memory remain living elements of Rapa Nui culture.
